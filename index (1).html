01 Procurement Spend & Supplier Risk Analysis

I analyzed 150 purchase orders from 10 suppliers to show where $759.6K was spent, which suppliers are the biggest delivery risks, and where combining orders could save roughly $10K to $20K (a planning estimate).

Project Overview & Methodology

I analyzed 150 purchase orders from 10 suppliers across 7 categories (Jan 5 to Oct 30, 2026) and built an interactive dashboard with filters for spend, lead time, on-time delivery and a supplier risk table. I then wrote an executive summary covering where the money goes, which suppliers are risky, and what a junior buyer or analyst should do next.

Spend is concentrated: Packaging, Ingredients and Cartons make up 66.9% of the $759.6K total, and three suppliers (Northstar, Crestline and Ironwood) make up about half. I found three groups of orders that could be combined (plastic packaging, ingredients and chemicals, and paper, cartons and labels), covering $506.6K or 67% of spend. The 2% to 4% savings rate is my own assumption, not a supplier quote.

Key Takeaways & What I Learned

136 of 150 orders (90.7%) arrived on time, but the 14 late orders were worth $48.4K. Sunrise Paper Products had the worst record at 3 of 16 orders late, and Apex Plastics and Meridian Labels & Print each had 2 of 15 late. Every one of the 36 item types comes from a single supplier, so there is no backup if one slips.

Late orders actually had shorter lead times (14.1 days) than on-time ones (16.8 days), which made me wonder whether we reorder too late on our side. I also learned to flag data quality issues, such as four PO numbers that appear twice, and to be upfront about assumptions and what the data cannot show.
